Step 4: Create interactive visualizations¶

In [15]:
audience_mapping = {
    'TV-MA': 'Adult (17+)', 'R': 'Adult (17+)', 'NC-17': 'Adult (17+)',
    'TV-14': 'Teens (14+)', 'PG-13': 'Teens (14+)',
    'TV-PG': 'Older Kids / Family', 'PG': 'Older Kids / Family',
    'TV-Y7': 'Young Children', 'TV-Y': 'Young Children', 'G': 'Young Children', 'TV-G': 'Young Children'
}
df['audience_segment'] = df['rating'].map(audience_mapping).fillna('Unrated/Other')

plt.style.use("dark_background")
plt.figure(figsize=(10, 5))

sns.countplot(
    data=df, 
    y="audience_segment", 
    hue="type", 
    order=['Adult (17+)', 'Teens (14+)', 'Older Kids / Family', 'Young Children', 'Unrated/Other'],
    palette="muted"
)

plt.title("Platform Catalog Strategy by Target Audience Segment", fontsize=14, pad=15)
plt.xlabel("Volume of Titles")
plt.ylabel("Target Audience")
plt.tight_layout()

plt.show()

Step 5: Present audience insights¶

• Grouped Countplot (Ratings by Type): Visually confirms that TV-MA is the tallest bar across the entire dataset. It also highlights structural boundaries—showing that R and PG-13 bars belong strictly to Movies, while TV-14 dominates TV Shows. • Exploded Genre Bar Chart (Genres): Reveals that the platform relies on hybrid stories (e.g., combining International with Crime or Drama). The high volume bars show a strategy engineered to appeal to multiple taste clusters at once. • Audience Segment Chart (Target Markets): Displays a steep pyramid shape. The Adult (17+) segment forms the massive base of the catalog, while Teens fill the middle tier, and Young Kids represent a minor sliver at the top.
